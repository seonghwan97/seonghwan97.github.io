Camera-Only Worker Monitoring with Pose-Derived Workload and Ergonomic Risk
  • Built a camera-only worker monitoring system that runs on ordinary site video with no wearables. A YOLO pose model extracts keypoints, ByteTrack holds each worker's identity across frames, and downstream heads roll activity, work state, and posture into a 10-second exposure score, so a safety lead reads one number per crew instead of watching four feeds. Holds a median 23 fps tracking 3 to 9 workers on an RTX 2060.
  • Checked each module against an independent reference across 42 subjects, reaching 0.861 activity accuracy, 0.775 work-state F1, and 0.793 correlation with a 3D-pose ergonomic reference. Workload only reached 0.526 against calorimetry, so I shipped it as an exposure indicator rather than a fatigue measurement.
  • Also built the CO2 sensing rig on Raspberry Pi and Jetson for the field cross-check. Presented at the BuiltWorlds 2026 Chicago Global Summit with a $12,500 research grant. Manuscript in preparation, so the code is not public yet.

Real-Time Control of Microfluidic Mixing from Image Measurements
  • Wrote the measurement code that registers and normalizes CFD contour images and experimental fluorescence images, then computes a 0 to 1 mixing index from pixel intensity along the outlet.
  • Benchmarked SVR, MLP, XGBoost, Random Forest, and linear regression as surrogates for the CFD solver. SVR won on held-out simulation data with MAE 0.0014 and R² 0.9998, putting error near a tenth of a percent of the index range, close enough to stand in for a full CFD solve during parameter search.
  • Ran inverse screening to find inlet conditions that hit a target outlet mixing value, then confirmed the picks with fresh CFD runs and six fluorescence experiments, so the chosen settings were checked against both simulation and the bench. Presented at the BMES Annual Meeting 2025.

Pipeline 1 / 2
Two-Stage Detection and Classification for Stacked Parcels
  • Two-stage recognition on photos of stacked parcels. DINO and YOLO detect product regions and Weighted Boxes Fusion merges them into cleaner boxes than either produced alone, then EfficientNet-B7 with test-time augmentation classifies each crop into a product name and category.
  • Output is one structured record per product, holding name, IoU, and box coordinates, which the counting step reads directly.
  • Placed 1st of 19 teams ($15,000), identifying 56.6% of the 53 evaluation products against 49.1% for the runner-up, at 0.80 mean IoU. No other team paired the top recognition rate with localization above 0.75 IoU.
